# Game Design

## North Star

Wall of Dead 2 is survival horror, not an arcade shooter. The player is under-supplied, afraid to waste ammo, and trying to reach a freedom gate through a ruined city that feels inhabited by things just off-screen. The game begins outside, with the player approaching the still-lit intake road to a place that was supposed to save people. The flashlight is a reliable tool; darkness comes from authored blackouts, dead rooms, and failing fixtures, not a battery meter.

## Player Loop

1. Enter an authored zone.
2. Read the space through light, sound, props, and silhouettes.
3. Decide whether to spend resources, sneak past, run, or interact.
4. Take a route forward and carry the consequences into the next zone.

## First Campaign Story Spine

- Intake road: approach the Freedom Gate lights, hear the broken public address system, and enter Building A before the road goes dark.
- Building A intake lobby: learn that survivors were funneled below, not sheltered; first notes and false silhouettes, with no direct enemy.
- Staging garage: first firearm pickup, delayed first contact, route choice, and aisle-by-aisle blackout.
- Triage pharmacy: shotgun pickup, clinical curtains, one blackout reveal, and a forced push through the triage bay.
- Greenline platform: flare pickup and an authored chase that should be escaped, not cleared.
- Gate maintenance corridor: close fire doors and shutters behind you while something forces the corridor open from the rear.
- Gate motor boiler room: pipe bomb pickup, environmental pressure, and a brute run-now moment.
- Gate crypt: Patient Zero is revealed as the thing guarding the mechanism while the gate motor opens.
- Freedom gate: the wall opens, but the broadcast continues beyond it.

## Scare Flow

- Build with space, sound, and objective changes before spawning anything.
- Use short cinematic beats to point the player toward the next threat or piece of story.
- Enemies should be rare. One reveal should change the player's plan.
- Chases should be explicit in the objective text and should not reward standing still to clear the room.
- Fights should feel loud and consequential, especially in the final gate crypt.

## Tone Rules

- Few enemies can be scarier than many.
- A gunshot should briefly make the player feel strong, then make the world feel worse.
- The flashlight should not run out; the building should decide when the room stops helping you.
- Narrative fragments should imply a larger catastrophe without stopping play.
